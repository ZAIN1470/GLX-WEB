Craftsmanship

312 hours, one pair of hands.

A GLX watch is not passed down a line. One watchmaker owns it from the first bridge to the final timing report, and signs it.

From bench to wrist

Three stages, no shortcuts.

  1. Movement

    Plates and bridges are machined in the Jura, then finished here by hand. Each edge is bevelled and polished with pegwood and diamond paste until it holds a clean line under the loupe.

  2. Case and dial

    Cases are cut from solid gold or steel and polished in stages, alternating brushed and mirror surfaces. Dials get their colour from layered lacquer, each coat cured before the next.

  3. Regulation

    The finished watch is timed in six positions and two temperatures over twenty-one days. If it drifts more than four seconds a day, it goes back to the bench.

Calibre G-07

An engine we are proud to show.

Every GLX has a sapphire caseback, because the movement is the point. The rotor is skeletonised and engraved, and the balance wheel beats at 28,800 vibrations an hour.

Materials

What we use, and what we refuse.

Our gold is recycled and traceable to the refinery. Our steel is 904L, which resists corrosion better than the grades most brands use. Our crystals are sapphire, front and back, with anti-reflective coating on the inside only so the outer face stays hard.

We do not use plated cases, mineral glass, or leather that has been sprayed to look like something more expensive. If a material cannot last fifty years, it does not go on a GLX.
